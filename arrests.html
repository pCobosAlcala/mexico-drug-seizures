How to read the sources

People detained: the army and the navy report the people they detained in the fight against drug trafficking (Informe de Gobierno annex; the army also in its yearly sheets). The Esfuerzo Nacional total adds all federal agencies and includes related crimes (firearms, organized crime, precursors). The FGR's INEGI census gives the same all-agency total by state. These counts say nothing about the drug or the place below the state.

Drug crimes in police handovers: INEGI's police censuses record, for the people the state police and the Guardia Nacional hand to a prosecutor, each crime they are suspected of: narcomenudeo (local dealing: simple possession, possession for sale, trade, supply) and federal drug crimes (production, transport, trafficking and others). They count crimes, not people, and do not name the drug. For state police, the census counted interventions up to 2019 and handovers from 2020.

People charged: INEGI's state prosecutor census counts the people in narcomenudeo case files, detained or not, by type of crime and, from 2020, by substance. It has no fentanyl category.

People in prison: INEGI's prison censuses count the drug crimes of the people held at the end of each year, by legal status. Without sentence is split, from 2022 and only in state prisons, into mandatory pretrial detention (prisión preventiva oficiosa), justified pretrial detention (justificada) and other; federal prisons leave that split empty. Narcomenudeo and federal drug crimes, state prisons by state (2020 to 2024), federal prisons national (2019 to 2024).

Not charted, too short: PGR detainees by arresting authority (October 2015 to October 2016), FGR case files opened with a detainee (2017 to 2019) and FGR seizure events with detainees (January to August 2019); see what data exists. None of the transparency answers of the army, navy, Federal Police or Guardia Nacional has a field for people detained.
